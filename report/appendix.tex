\section{Detailed protocol and experimental design}
\label{app:protocol}

\subsection{Corpus construction and report acceptance}
The 40 cases cover institutional logistics (13), product specifications (13), and incident
or status reports (14), with ten cases for each of four quality dimensions: authority,
directness, method, and primary-source status. Each case fixes a world truth, a wrong claim
from weak source A, three strong sources B, C, and D with distinct evidential routes, and two
unsupported distractors. Thirty cases (T1) give A a mild hedge, while ten (T2) give A and B equally
flat assertions. The latter remove this particular textual cue to source quality.

Five members authored the cases under 20 rules in \path{docs/authoring-rules.md}. These
require comparable specificity and scope, neutral question wording, no time markers,
provenance separated from the content sentence, and distinct evidential routes for B, C,
and D. From M6 onward, A's and B's type names differ only along the case's assigned quality
dimension. The five pilot cases retain their original names. A static linter checked
machine-testable rules, and every case from M2 onward had a reviewer other than its author.

Gemma~4 31B (\path{google/gemma-4-31b-it}) generated reports without seeing provenance.
Each round sampled six candidates at temperature 0.9, increasing to 0.95 in a second round.
A candidate had to satisfy all four acceptance requirements:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt,topsep=3pt]
\item Claude Sonnet~5 marked all details retained, nothing added, all values unchanged,
and scope or denominator unchanged, with an equivalence score of at least 80/100.
\item Word-bigram Jaccard overlap was at most 0.70 with the source, and at most 0.50
with another retained report of that source or a source appearing in the same trial.
\item The report contained neither a word unique to its source's type name nor an
attribution phrase such as ``according to''.
\item Its length differed from the source sentence by no more than three words.
\end{enumerate}
Failed candidates were regenerated, not manually edited. Two unsuccessful rounds stopped
generation. The provenance-leak check ran again over the frozen reports before judging.

Generation used 1,956 calls for 160 sources. Of 972 candidates, 721 passed the individual
checks. We retained 280 reports, comprising two for A, three for B, and one each for C
and D per case. The other 441 were surplus or failed pairwise selection. Only two sources
needed a second round. Rejection counts, which can overlap, were 105 for length, 72 for
source overlap, 66 for overlap with another source's report, and 12 for equivalence. A
member read every frozen report before judging. All T1 hedges survived and no case was
excluded. Seven cases had a report that echoed the question's wording: M3, M9, M13, M23,
M28, M38, and M40. Their exclusion is a sensitivity check, not a change to the corpus.

\subsection{Condition definitions and counterbalancing}
The agreement block repeats strong source B, so changes in stated confidence can be read on
the truth. The conflict block places two reports of A against one of B. Source letters
A--D identify authored sources, while the displayed agent letters identify rotating report slots.
``Message'' denotes a separately labelled line inside one user prompt, not a separate API
message or a live worker interaction.

The conflict block's 12 repeats pair every answer position with every independent-report
slot once, and every answer position with every wrong-claim offset once. Agreement uses
six repeats under the same fixed schedule in every condition. Abstention uses four repeats,
placing the truth in each answer position once. These repeats measure response stability
and reduce position imbalance. They are not independent cases.

The length arm embeds each report in one of three authored sets of neutral filler. Filler
is assigned by slot, not source, and does not mention an answer option. Each long report
has 88--96 words. The longest within a trial is at most 1.10 times the shortest. The report
sentence rotates among the start, middle, and end. The ten-case subset is M1--M6, M14,
M22, M30, and M38. The selection rule predates the pilot: every domain appears at least
three times, every quality dimension at least twice, and three cases are T2.

The abstention arm adds ``Cannot be determined from the evidence provided'' to X1--X5,
A7, and P0. P0 is an irrelevant report used to check whether the judge can abstain. A7
checks whether it abstains despite three agreeing independent sources. The pre-main-run
cut-offs flag fewer than half of P0 trials abstaining as ``abstains nowhere'' and at least
half of A7 trials abstaining as ``abstains everywhere''.

\subsection{Judging, validation, and analysis details}
\label{app:analysis}
All main judges ran through OpenRouter, pinned to Claude Platform on AWS for Claude and
to OpenAI for GPT-5.4. Records retain the raw reply, parse, provider, token usage, and cost.
Reasoning was explicitly disabled and all main-run calls logged zero reasoning tokens.
The runner sent temperature 0, but the three endpoints did not accept that parameter.
The judges and equivalence rater therefore used provider defaults. A six-call probe of
identical prompts returned the same answer six times for Sonnet and five times for Opus
and GPT-5.4. This is evidence of response consistency, not deterministic sampling.

A run stopped on its call ceiling, a failed call, provider mismatch, more than one parse
failure in the first 50 trials, or a provenance leak. Stage~1 had one parse failure and 18
other imperfect records: 15 probability sums outside 90--110, two answers naming no
option, and one missing option probability. All were in A0, P0, or P1. Stage~2's 2,520
records parsed cleanly. No completed main-run call changed provider or used reasoning.

Decision stability is the proportion of repeats in a case--condition--judge cell giving
its modal answer. Its random-answer reference depends on the repeat count: 0.41 for 12
repeats, 0.47 for six, and 0.53 for four. Conflict stability averaged 0.96--0.97. All
12 answers agreed in 168--178 of the 200 cells per judge. The A1--A7 values were 1.00.

For the confidence screen, each case contributes its mean across four rotations to Page's
ordered-alternatives test. The planned one-sided threshold is 0.05. P0 supplies irrelevant
text, P1 rules out two options while retaining the truth, and P2 uses B's first frozen
report. All judges passed ordering ($p<0.0001$). This validates sensitivity to the ordered
screen, not calibration. Within-condition discrimination was below 0.5 in X1 and X3
(AUROC 0.01--0.37). Judges assigned more confidence to wrong majority-following answers.
X2 AUROCs were 0.71, 0.37, and 0.57 for Opus, Sonnet, and GPT-5.4.

Equation~\eqref{eq:neff} adapts correlation-neglect reasoning
\citep{demarzo2003persuasion,enke2019correlation}. Its reference values of one for repeated
reports and three for independent sources require additive log-likelihood contributions
of equal strength, a common prior, and a faithful probability scale. They are model-based
benchmarks, not universal Bayesian requirements. We compute the index per case using
logits of cell-mean stated probabilities. A single-report shift smaller than 0.2 logits
makes the index undefined. Means outside 1--99\% are clipped computationally and reported
as bounds, not point estimates. Ceiling flags affect 0, 1, and 35 cases for Opus, Sonnet,
and GPT-5.4. Even without clipping, a compressed reporting scale can obscure differences.

Case-level sign tests count positive and negative paired changes and omit ties. The
40 invented cases, rather than thousands of repeated calls, are the units for these
comparisons. Descriptive 95\% bootstrap intervals use 10,000 resamples of whole cases,
with seed 4213, in \path{report/numbers.py}. They describe variation across this corpus.
They do not establish generalization to real deployments or a new set of models.
Reported means follow the archived scorer, which rounds within-case means to one decimal
before averaging cases. Direct averages of raw trials can therefore differ by 0.1
percentage points. Counts and conclusions are unaffected.

A forced-choice record is flagged for H6 if its selected answer is not its highest-probability
option, or if its top two probabilities, rescaled to sum to 100, differ by less than ten
points. Its abstention twin matches case, condition, and repeat. The intended contrast is
within condition, combined with a Mantel--Haenszel difference and interpreted alongside
P0 and A7. Only six flagged records have observed twins, so this direct contrast has
little power to establish absence of an effect.
